Chapter 1 — Arrival

Ghana.
Age 14.
Germany.

My German was almost zero. I remember sitting in class understanding maybe every fifth word. I smiled a lot because I didn't know what else to do. People thought I was confident.

1 in 5
words she understood
in class that first year
I was terrified.— Dr. Amara Osei
2
Chapter 2 — Her Mother

She broke
down.

Work stress. Isolation. The weight of being an immigrant woman with no support system. In our community — you prayed, you kept going, you didn't talk about it. I watched her suffer in silence.

Someone should have been there for her. Someone who understood where she came from.
I decided to become that person.— Dr. Amara Osei
Chapter 3 — The Room

First lecture. She counted.

47
students
in the room.
She was the only Black woman.

The professor was explaining Freud and she remembers thinking — none of this was written for me, or about me, or with me in mind.

I almost left after the first semester.
Chapter 4 — The Rejection

Her supervisor called her research on mental health in African diaspora communities —

“too niche.”

She went home and cried for two days.
Then she rewrote it. Made it stronger.

The result:

Published.
He came to congratulate me. I thanked him — because that rejection made me fight harder.— Dr. Amara Osei

Chapter 5 — What She Knows Now
Being the only one
in the room
is not a sign
you don't belong.
It is a sign the room
hasn't caught up yet.
Find your people early. Not the people who look like you necessarily — but the people who genuinely see you. Even one person who sees you changes everything.— Dr. Amara Osei
She says this — for you
“If you leave, they win twice.
Once because you left —
and once because the next girl
who looks like you
won't see you there
when she arrives.”
— Dr. Amara Osei, Clinical Psychologist
